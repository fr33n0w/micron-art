Micron Artist

What it is for

Headers and lettering, the part of a NomadNet page that is painful to colour by hand. Paste ASCII art, choose how it should be coloured, copy the micron out. It does one thing: the art block.

Whole pages belong in Micron Composer, which is built for page structure: paste the header from here at the top and write the rest of the page around it.

How to use it

  1. Paste ASCII art into the first box, or press Demo page to load the title card and experiment on that.
  2. Pick a palette and a background, then try the effects. Magic rolls the whole lot at once, usually as a named look (crt, neon, poster, heat...) and sometimes inventing a palette from a colour harmony; Shuffle redraws the random colours.
  3. Copy the micron from the last box and paste it into your .mu page.

Keeping a style you like

The Recipe box at the bottom holds every setting in one line. Copy it when a look works, then paste it back later, with different ASCII art in the box, to dress that art the same way. It carries the random seed too, so even the random colours come back identical.

If the art looks misaligned

Both boxes are drawn by a browser, with browser fonts and browser metrics. NomadNet draws the same text in a terminal. Shade blocks (░ ▒ ▓ █) and box-drawing borders are the usual suspects: if a font lacks them the browser borrows them from another face at another width, and the art drifts sideways a little more with every one of them on the line. Frames drawn with the Frame button show this most.

The text itself is fine. Do not fix it by deleting spaces, that damages the real thing. Try another font from the picker next to the preview first, and test the page in NomadNet. The same art that looks broken in one editor renders perfectly in Micron Composer and in a terminal.

Micron notes

Classic colours are 12 bit, three hex digits: `F0f0 sets the foreground, `B002 the background, `f and `b close them. Every reader understands them.

24 bit writes `FT00ff00 and `BT000022, six digits, sixteen million colours and smooth gradients. NomadNet reads them since 0.9.11 and shows them exactly with colormode = 24bit in its config; on 256 colours it picks the nearest. An older reader prints the tags as text. Every colour change costs bytes, so steps sets how many shades a gradient may use: fewer steps, smaller page.

Backticks and backslashes in your art are escaped automatically. Line spacing is a preview control only, micron has no such tag: the reader's terminal decides it.
